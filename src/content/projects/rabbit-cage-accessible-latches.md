## Overview

A three-concept accessibility project: our client uses a wheelchair and has limited finger dexterity, so the small spring-loaded latches on his rabbit's cage were unusable without help. I designed two of the three solutions our team proposed — a durable wooden lever latch and a solenoid-actuated lock driven by a large rocker switch. A teammate developed the third concept.

## Wooden lever latch

The mechanical concept. A pivoting hardwood lever swings through a wide arc to disengage a hook from the cage door — wide arc means lower precision force, and the lever sits at chair-height so the client can engage it with the side of his hand instead of pinching with fingertips. Turned the pivot collar on a manual wood lathe; the rest is laser-cut and chamfered for comfort.

## Solenoid lock

The electronic concept. A 12 V solenoid bolts to the cage frame and retracts a pin when energized. Activation comes from a large rocker switch mounted within easy reach — no fine motor control required, no holding pressure to keep it open. Simple driver circuit (relay, flyback diode, 12 V supply), housed in a 3D-printed enclosure that velcros to the side of the cage.

## Challenges and learnings

The hardest part of an accessibility project isn't the engineering — it's getting the use case right. Our first wooden-lever sketch required the client to push *down* on the lever, which assumed he could lean his weight onto it. A short conversation revealed he couldn't, and the redesign turned it into a horizontal motion he could do seated. Lesson: prototype against the actual person, not the imagined person, as early as possible.

## Outcome

Both designs delivered to the client. The solenoid version became his daily driver; the wooden latch is the backup for when the cage gets moved out to the patio and the wiring isn't convenient.
