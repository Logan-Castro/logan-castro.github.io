## Overview

A folding, full-scale nap pod prototype for sleep-deprived college students. The pod opens into a reclined chair shaped around the NASA neutral body position — the joint angles a human body settles into in microgravity, which turn out to be close to what your spine actually wants when you sleep upright. Closed, it folds flat enough to live in a campus lounge without dominating the space. Took second place in the popular vote.

## Research

Started with a survey of how undergrads at BC were sleeping — answer: badly, and almost never during the actual day. The nap-pod concept came from the gap between "I'm exhausted between classes" and "the only places to sleep are dorms and library carrels." NASA's neutral body position diagram set the joint-angle targets so the geometry wasn't a guess.

## Build

CAD in Fusion 360, with Slicer for Fusion to break the curved ribs into laser-cut plywood profiles. The fold mechanism is a four-bar linkage that lets the back panel rotate into the seat panel and lock flat. Built full-scale so judges could actually sit in it — there's a noticeable difference between a poster mockup and a chair you can put your whole body weight into.

## Outcome

Full deliverable package: renderings, presentation poster, and the working prototype. Second place in the popular vote, but more useful was the feedback at the demo: every judge sat in it, and the ones who did were the ones who voted for it.
